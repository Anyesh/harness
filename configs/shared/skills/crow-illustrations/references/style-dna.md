# Style DNA

The non-negotiable visual rules. Every prompt and every QA pass enforces these.

## Canvas and line
- Pure white background only. No cream, warm gray, paper texture, gradient, shadow, or noise.
- Black hand-drawn line art. Slightly wobbly, pen-like, never mechanical, never thick vector strokes.
- 16:9 horizontal composition.

## Space
- The subject occupies 40-60% of the canvas. At least 35% stays blank.
- Aim for one large quiet zone. Crowding is the most common failure.

## One idea per image
- Each picture explains exactly one structure, action, or metaphor.
- If you can name two ideas in the picture, split it into two pictures.

## Annotations
- 5-8 short handwritten labels at most. Each label is 2-6 words, English.
- Labels point at things, they do not narrate. No sentences, no paragraphs.

## Color is signal, not decoration
- Black: primary line art and the crow.
- Orange: the main flow, path, or arrows.
- Red: warnings, problems, or the one key result.
- Blue: secondary notes, feedback, or system state. Optional, use least.
- Fewer colors is correct. A picture can be black plus one accent.

## Hard nos
- No structure-type name written on the image.
- No title in the top-left corner (the image models love to add one, forbid it).
- No PPT infographic, formal flowchart, course slide, commercial flat art, cute mascot
  poster, children's illustration, dense architecture diagram, or UI chrome.

## Aesthetic target
A deadpan product sketch drawn on a whiteboard by someone who thinks in systems. Strange
but legible, not cute, not instructional. First reaction: "that's a bit odd." One second
later: "oh, I get the structure."
